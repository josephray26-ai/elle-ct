Passage I

THE LIBRARY ON WHEELS

Every Tuesday morning, a repurposed school bus rolls to a stop at the corner
of Marshall and Ninth. Painted deep green and lettered by hand, it carries
roughly four thousand books, a folding table, and Ruth Alvarez, who has driven
the county's mobile library for nineteen years. The bus reaches neighborhoods
that the main branch, eleven miles away, has never served well. On a good
morning, forty children climb the steps before noon.

Ruth keeps no formal schedule for what she brings. She watches what leaves the
shelves and she restocks accordingly. When a boy asked for a book about
volcanoes and she had none, she returned the following week with four.
